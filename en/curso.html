Lesson 1 of 10

An animation made from a request

You can paste a ready-made request into an AI chat and get a 10-second animation without opening any editing software.

A still post gets lost among all the videos. Ordering an animation costs a lot and takes days. Today, you can ask for one in writing and get the finished animation in the conversation.

In 1 minute

  1. The AI writes drawing instructions for each frame. It’s not an invented video.
  2. You describe it, the AI draws and checks it. You make the final call.
  3. A good request has four parts. Today, you’ll use a ready-made one; in lesson 2, you’ll understand each part.

1The animation is drawn, not filmed

Claude Opus 5.5 doesn’t create a video like a camera. It writes drawing instructions. The browser reads those instructions and draws each frame, many times per second.

Because it’s a set of instructions, the result can be repeated. The same instructions always draw the same animation, with the letters spelled correctly.

A baker wanted to show the three steps of an order: order, oven, and delivery. The animation below came from a single request. It’s running now, on this page.

2Drawing from a request is different from generated video

Some tools invent video from a sentence. They work well for scenes with people and landscapes. For text, logos, and numbers, drawing from a request usually works better.

A real estate agent needed an opening with the agency’s name. In the generated video, the name came out with the letters mixed up. In the drawn animation, the name was spelled correctly.

3Paste the request and the animation appears in the conversation

Everything happens in the Claude chat, in your browser. Choose the Opus 5.5 model in the selector below the message box. Paste the request and send it. If Opus 5.5 doesn’t appear, your account may not have access to it: use the most advanced model on the list.

The animation appears right in the conversation, in a frame you can tap to open. It keeps spinning: the end connects to the beginning. This is called a loop.

Stuck here? That's normalIf the animation doesn't appear, write "show the animation in a frame I can open here in the conversation". If it appears still, ask "make it loop".

It's just a conversation: nothing is published. If it looks odd, send the same request again in a new conversation. The technical lines at the end, such as render(t) and 1920x1080, are instructions for the AI: copy them without changing anything.

Lesson 2 of 10

Four parts: R, I, S, and E

You can write an animation request from scratch using the four parts of the RISE method for something you need at work.

In lesson 1, you used a ready-made request. When you write your own, it’s easy to mix everything into one sentence. Then AI guesses what you didn’t say, and guesses wrong.

In 1 minute

  1. R for References: where the colors, lettering, and logo come from.
  2. I de Ideia: começo, uma mudança e fim. S de eStilo (do inglês style): como parece e como se move.
  3. E for Examine: AI checks its own work before handing it over.

1R: the reference guides AI; AI doesn’t make things up

The reference is what AI should follow exactly. Without it, AI chooses “pretty” colors and lettering that aren’t yours.

A real estate agent asked for “something that looks like my real estate agency.” It came back in blue and gold; his brand is green. The second time, he pasted the color codes and attached the logo.

2I: a beginning, one change, and an ending

A short animation is a story in three moments. What appears first, the one thing that changes, and how it ends. If it’s a loop, the ending becomes the beginning again.

Just one change. When everything moves at once, your eyes don’t know where to look.

A baker wanted a post about the cake of the week. First she asked for “cake, confetti, price, stars, and sparkle.” Then she asked for just one change: the icing drips down and reveals the price.

3S: how it looks and how it moves

Style has two parts. Appearance: background, colors, lettering, and texture. And movement: fast or slow, what moves first, and how smoothly it moves.

The same image can feel like a different piece when the movement changes. That’s why you describe the movement too.

The designer wrote "come in slowly, like a door opening." The real estate agency's opening stayed calm, just like the way they serve clients.

4E: the AI checks before delivering

The last part tells the AI to look at its own result. It draws five still frames: 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of the animation. It looks at each one, fixes anything that looks wrong, and draws it again.

This line goes in every request and is almost always the same. It has technical terms, such as render(t) and 1920x1080 (the size of a landscape screen). These are instructions for the AI: copy them without changing anything. Lesson 3 shows you how to check them yourself.

Test yourself

The animation came back with the brand colors, but five things move at the same time. Which part of the request would you rewrite?

Stuck here? That's normalDon't know the color code? Write the color in words ("the dark green on the building's front") and attach a photo. In lesson 4, you'll learn how to get the exact code.

Practice now 0/3

Write your RISE request

You're done when the animation comes back with your colors and just one change. On a computer, about 10 minutes.

Nothing is published. If it looks strange, change just one line and send it again so you know which line made the difference.

R: <attach a photo or screenshot, or write the colors and lettering you use>. Use these exactly.
I: A <10>-second loop. Start: <what appears first>. Middle: <the one change>. End: <how it ends>. The last frame is the same as the first.
S: Look: <background>, <main color>, one <color> accent, real texture. Movement: <slow and smooth, or fast and sharp>; <what> moves first. 1080x1080.
E: Draw every frame in code from a single render(t) function. Add real texture to make it look handmade. Before finishing, check the frames at 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%, and fix anything that's wrong.
See the template filled in by a baker

I: 8 seconds. Start: the whole cake. Middle: the sauce drips down and reveals "R$ 89". End: the sauce goes back up.

Five frames, ten mistakes

You can check an animation using five still frames, find a mistake, and ask for the right fix in one sentence.

The animation moves quickly and looks good. The flaw shows up later, when a client pauses the video and sees a cut-off letter. Checking it while paused takes three minutes and avoids that embarrassment.

In 1 minute

  1. Ask to see the animation paused at five moments: 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%.
  2. Check each frame against a list of ten common mistakes.
  3. Found one? Say which frame, what’s wrong, and how it should look.

1See the animation paused

In motion, your eyes forgive almost anything. Paused, they don’t. That’s why the check uses five frozen frames, spread from beginning to end.

You don’t need to pause at the right time. Ask AI to make a strip with the five frames below the animation.

A baker made an animation of sauce revealing the price. She asked for the strip and saw that, in the 50% frame, the sauce partly covered the price.

2A real example of a mistake

This mistake happened while preparing this course. In an animation with letters, the red disk passed over the word. In motion, it was barely noticeable. In the 50% frame, it was obvious.

A real estate agent checked the agency’s opening animation the same way. In the 25% frame, the phone appeared for only half a second. The agent asked for it to stay on screen longer.

3Ten mistakes that make it look amateurish

Look at each frame for these ten things. Over time, you’ll spot them all at a glance.

Want to know why the animation always repeats the same way?

The request says to draw everything "from a function of time." At the same moment, you get the same frame. That's why the preview strip shows exactly what the client will see. If the texture were chosen at random each time, the previewed frame would be different from the published one.

4Ask for the correction with three details

"It looks strange" makes the AI guess. Say which frame, what's wrong, and how it should look. Then ask for the strip again so you can check the correction.

Stuck here? That's normalDon't know what to call the problem? Write what you see: "in the 75% frame, the bottom of the letter looks cut off." The AI understands the description.

The slide and page that come to life

You can turn a slide you already have into a 10-second loop using your exact brand colors.

You already have slides and a presentation page. Recreating everything as an animation sounds like weeks of work. It isn’t: the existing slide becomes the request’s reference.

In 1 minute

  1. Take a screenshot of the slide and attach it to the request: that’s the R.
  2. For the exact colors, ask AI to read your page and list the codes.
  3. On the page, the text stays as text; only the decoration becomes animated.

1The slide you already have is the reference

Open the slide and take a screenshot. On Windows, press Windows + Shift + S and select. On Mac, Command + Shift + 4. Then drag the image into the message box.

A real estate agent had a slide with the three steps of buying a home: visit, offer, and deed. They attached the screenshot and asked for a beam of light to travel along the steps.

2The exact colors come from your page

Every webpage stores its colors as a color code. With it, the animation’s green matches the site’s green.

You don’t need to search for them. Ask AI to open the page and list what it finds.

A baker asked AI to do this for her ordering page. She got the list and pasted it into the R for her next requests.

Stuck here? That's normalDon’t have your own page, or can’t AI open it? Take a screenshot of your social media profile or one of your materials and write "read the colors in this screenshot." The code may be approximate; check it against the site.

3If you have a page, the text stays as text

The animation can also go at the top of your page. One rule prevents headaches: the title, sentence, and button stay as real text. Only the decoration becomes animated.

At the top of the order page, the baker asked for sprinkles that fall slowly behind the title "Orders open." The title stayed as text, and she can change the phrase whenever she wants.

Practice now 0/3

Add motion to one of your slides

You’re done when your slide is playing in a loop, with your text and colors. On a computer, about 10 minutes.

The original slide doesn’t change: you only send a copy as an image. Don’t have a slide ready? Use a photo of your own poster or printed page.

R: Here is my slide (screenshot attached). Keep the same layout and the same text. Colors: <paste the color list, if you have one>.

S: The slide’s own colors, with a single highlight. Slow, gentle motion; the last frame is the same as the first. 1920x1080.
E: Draw every frame in code from a single render(t) function. Add real texture so it looks handmade. Before you finish, check the frames at 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%, and fix anything that’s wrong.

Reel with captions that light up

You can request a reel with animation on top, your video underneath, and captions that light up word by word, then download the result to post.

Videos of people talking hold attention better when something on screen reinforces what they’re saying. Putting this together in an editor takes an afternoon. With one request, the animation and captions are made together.

In 1 minute

  1. The vertical screen is split: animation on top, you underneath.
  2. The captions show up to three words, and the spoken word lights up.
  3. Ask for two buttons on the page: one to choose your video and another to download the finished reel.

1The screen split into two halves

On your phone, the video is vertical. The bottom half shows you speaking. The top half shows an animation that illustrates what you’re saying.

A baker recorded ten seconds saying that party orders need to be placed seven days in advance. Above, a calendar flips through the days until it marks day 7. The example below is running.

2Word-by-word captions

The captions show up to three words at a time. The word being spoken lights up in a highlight color. To do this, the AI needs the timing for each word.

The simplest way: paste the text you said and give the video’s duration. The AI divides the time among the words.

Want more precise captions?

Video editing apps on your phone that create automatic captions usually export a file ending in .srt. It’s just text, with the timing for each sentence. Open it, copy everything, and paste it into the conversation along with your request.

Stuck here? That's normalDid the caption appear too early? Write "the word 'seven' lights up too early; delay it by half a second". Check the five-frame strip (lesson 3).

3The first version is crowded

A candid note: in tests of this method, the first reel came out too busy. Too many things appeared and disappeared, and one scribbly letter was hard to read. One sentence fixes it.

A real estate agent made a reel for an apartment. In the first version, the floor plan, price, and neighborhood competed for space in the top half. They asked for just one idea: the floor plan drawing itself.

4From chat to post

The animation starts as a page. To post it, you need a video file. Ask for a button that records the ten seconds and saves the file.

Two ways around it, if needed. If the button won’t download the file in the conversation, open the animation panel, download the page, and open it in your browser. If the social media app rejects the file, use your phone’s screen recorder while the animation plays.

One scene, three formats

You can request the same animation in horizontal, vertical, and square formats, with the composition rearranged for each one, without anything being cut off.

Each place calls for a different format. Horizontal for the big screen, vertical for the phone, square for the feed. Cropping cuts off the title; making it three times takes all day.

In 1 minute

  1. Cropping cuts off the edges. Rearranging rebuilds the composition for the new format.
  2. Ask for one scene that rearranges itself into 16:9, 9:16, and 1:1.
  3. Put the word that changes on a separate line at the top of the recipe, so you can change it later without starting over.

1Cropping isn't adapting

The three formats are named by their proportions. 16:9, 9:16, and 1:1. When you only crop, whatever was at the edges disappears.

A real estate agent cropped the landscape opening to fit the portrait format. The real estate agency’s name and the last step disappeared. They asked to rearrange it instead of cropping it.

2See the same animation in all three formats

The animation from lesson 1 was requested with the ability to rearrange itself in mind. Below, it runs in three sizes at the same time. Notice the timeline: landscape in the wide format, portrait on a phone, and square.

A baker uses the same trick: the seven-day deadline animation is portrait for a phone and square for the feed.

3Keep the changing word at the top

One detail in the request saves a lot of work. Ask for the main word to be on its own line at the top of the recipe. Then, for a new version, you only need to change that word.

Stuck here? That's normalIf one of the formats has something cut off, ask for the five-frame strip for just that format: "show the five frames in 9:16". Fix that one without changing the others.

The logo in motion, with sound

You can make a 5-second intro with a real logo moving in a way that fits the brand, with a short sound synced to it.

Nothing gets a brand's attention like its own logo. Opening a presentation with the client's animated logo shows care. Before, this required an animator; now it fits in one request.

In 1 minute

  1. Always use the real logo file. Drawing it from memory gets it wrong.
  2. Ask what movement fits the brand, not which animation works for every brand.
  3. The sound also comes from the request, synced to the movement, starting silent.

1The real file, never from memory

If you ask for "the logo of such-and-such real estate company," the AI draws it from memory. It looks close, but it's wrong. Attach the file. PNG works; the best format is SVG.

A real estate agent was going to present properties to a construction company partner. On its website, he right-clicked the logo › Save image as. On a phone, tap and hold the logo › Save image. He attached the file to the request.

Stuck here? That's normalIf the website won't let you save the logo, ask the client for the file: "could you send me the logo as an SVG or a PNG with a transparent background?". Every company has one.

2What movement fits the brand?

Using the same animation for every logo makes them all look alike. The right question is: how does this brand move? A brand made of blocks builds itself. A music brand pulses. A sports brand streaks across the screen.

A baker's logo has a cupcake with frosting. She asked for the frosting to drip until it formed the name of her studio.

3The sound also comes from the request

The AI writes a short audio logo in the code itself, without downloading music from anywhere. The sound is tied to the motion: it plays when the animation reaches the agreed point.

Always ask for it to start muted, with a button to turn on the sound. Sound that starts on its own can bother people in a quiet place. See an audio logo like this in action and tap turn on sound.

Take the style, not the image

You can describe the style of a reference image and use that style in a new animation of your own.

You see a beautiful look and think: I want that. Copying the image won’t work because it belongs to someone else and says something different. What you want is its visual language.

In 1 minute

  1. Style is color palette, lettering, texture, composition, and movement.
  2. First, ask for a written description of the style. Only then create the animation.
  3. There are six ready-to-use styles in the course library, each with a complete request.

1Style is the language, not the drawing

A reference image has two layers: what it shows and how it shows it. You take only the second: the colors, lettering, texture, and the way things are arranged in the frame.

A real estate agent found a layered cut-paper poster with shadows on a design inspiration site. She wanted that style to show the view from an apartment.

2Write first, then create

The trick that improves the result the most: ask AI to describe the style before drawing. That way, it settles on the palette and lettering without changing them halfway through.

A pastry chef used a two-color print as a reference. The first version was almost right. One sentence fixed it: "add print grain and make the two colors slightly misaligned".

Stuck here? That's normalNot every reference comes out perfectly on the first try. If it’s "almost" right, don’t start over: say in one sentence what’s missing, such as "more grain" or "faded colors, like an old film".

3Six ready-to-use styles

The course library has six styles with complete requests: exact colors, lettering, timing, and rules. Replace the example word with your own. Below, style 03 in action.

Test with five, then scale up

You can plan a batch production: one template, one list, and a test with five pieces before making them all.

One piece turned out great. Now you need thirty: one for each product, property, or client. Making them one by one takes all week. Making all thirty at once without testing could repeat the same mistake thirty times.

In 1 minute

  1. Batch production has three parts: a list, a template, and a folder for the results.
  2. Test with five, check them, and only then make the rest.
  3. For dozens of pieces, a coding agent on your computer can do the heavy work.

1One list, one template, one folder

The animated template is the piece you’ve already approved, with the parts that change kept separate. The list says what goes into each piece. The folder holds the finished videos.

A real estate agent has thirty properties in their portfolio. They want an 8-second intro video for each one, with the neighborhood, price, and a photo of the building’s front.

2Five first, thirty later

The golden rule for any AI batch job: test, check, scale up. Five items reveal problems that only show up with real data. A long name that doesn’t fit. A light logo on a light background.

A baker did the same with twelve flavors. In the test of five, the flavor "dulce de leche with walnuts" didn’t fit. She asked for smaller text for long names before making all twelve.

3Who does the heavy lifting

In AI chat, you can make five or ten items. For dozens of videos, there’s a coding agent, such as Claude Code with Opus 5.5. It reads the list, fills in the template, records each video, and puts together a collage: one video showing all the intro videos side by side in a grid.

In AI chat, the process is the same on a smaller scale: "make the first five, one at a time, each with the download video button."

Stuck here? That's normalThe coding agent takes a little preparation on your computer, and you don’t need it to get something out of this lesson. The rule of testing with five works the same in AI chat: ask for five items, check them, then ask for the rest.

From request to small team

You can divide a piece into six roles, from director to reviewer, and use the same method for work that isn’t animation.

One request is enough for a simple piece. With a larger piece, everything gets mixed together: the message, colors, movement, and review. Real teams separate the work into roles.

In 1 minute

  1. Six roles: director, art director, animator, programmer, reviewer, and adjuster.
  2. The value is in the cycle: make, look, evaluate, correct, and make it again.
  3. The same template works for a report, page, ad, and lesson.

1Six roles, one piece

You can ask for each role in a separate conversation, or have the same AI take on one role at a time. What matters is that each one delivers something to the next.

A baker made a launch presentation this way. The director chose the phrase. The art director used the construction company’s colors. The reviewer found two text sections that were too fast.

2The value is in the review cycle

Generating is the easy part. What makes the piece good is going back: look at the result, compare it with what you agreed on, and make corrections. Repeat until it passes.

A baker started adding this request at the end: "if any flaws show up, correct them and check again before showing me". The pieces started arriving ready.

Stuck here? That's normalSix roles may seem like a lot for a small piece. You don’t have to use them: for a simple post, the RISE request does it all. Use the roles when the piece is big or important.

3The same template beyond animation

RISE teaches something that works for any request: say where the material comes from, what you want, the rules, and how to check it. A good request also explains when the work is ready.
